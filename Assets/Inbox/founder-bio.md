## C — LONG VERSION (for `/about` page)

### I build business machines.

By the time I was 22, I was running a distribution territory that moves around 8.5 crore taka a month.

Two brands. FMCG and telecom. Twenty-four people on the ground. Seven DSRs, three SRs, a warehouse, a depot, a fleet, and a thousand small decisions a day. From depot opening to the daily reconciliation, I make sure the thing runs.

But that wasn't the plan.

The plan started in a university hall in Khulna, watching case competitions get won and lost on the same three things: a strategy that could actually ship, a team that could execute it, and a story tight enough to make people care. I won a few of those. Then I won bigger ones.

Then I flew to Boston with Team Fortune 501 to represent Bangladesh at Hult Prize Global. We made the Top 20 of 5,000+ teams at Marico's Over The Wall. We were good at the slides. We were better at the systems behind them.

After that, one question stuck with me: why do most businesses run like they've never opened a spreadsheet?

So I started **BusinessDawg.**

Most agencies want to sell you a logo. Most consultants want to sell you a deck. I want to sell you the machine — the actual thing that turns inputs into customers into cash, on repeat. Branding, AI automation, web and product, growth, marketing infrastructure — those aren't service lines. They're parts. They snap together. You stack the ones you need. We build the ones you can't.

**On the side, I run a separate company** — **Shadai Ghar**, a direct-to-consumer grocery venture in Faridpur. Different brand, different team, different P&L. 500 families in six weeks. 23-27% gross margins. Real e-commerce platform behind it, built in Next.js, Postgres, payments, AI shopping recs — the whole stack.

It's not a hypothetical case study. It's a working machine running on real revenue. And it's the reason I'm not asking you to take advice from someone who's only ever made slides — I've spent my own money on the bet, and the machine works.

I'm Gen Z. I run a distribution business. I run a D2C startup. I write code with Claude and Codex sitting next to me. I've been in rooms with Unilever executives and in rooms with retailers at 7 a.m. I've learned the same thing in both: the people who win aren't the ones with the biggest idea. They're the ones with the working system.

That's what we build at BusinessDawg.
